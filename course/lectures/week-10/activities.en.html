    Week 10 studio: audit one instance

    The second session is a 90-minute studio on your own project. You audit one instance: one task that an agent attempted in your project, the check that decided it, and the record of its attempts. The lecture did this on an invented table. Here the record is yours, so the result is a statement about your project's evidence.

    Due this week: A6. Its rules and hand-in are on the assignment page. A6 asks for part of this audit on an instance the course supplies: a counterexample for its check, the complete denominator and the limits of the claim. A6's instance has its own format and words. It uses success and failure for pass and fail. It also has a missing row, where the table in step 5 below leaves the row out. audit.py does not read that format, so you recompute A6's counts yourself. A6 asks for at least three unsupported claims. This studio asks for two.

    Work individually, with a written alternative to any discussion. Follow the practices in Tools and model access. Nothing below calls a model. If you choose to run your agent again to add attempts, use your course seat as usual.

    Before you start

    Bring one instance. A good one is small and already has a check you can run:

    • a task from your project that your agent attempted, with the test or command that accepted or rejected the result; or
    • a change from one of your open-source contributions that a project check accepted.

    One recorded attempt is enough. If no check decided the task, pick another task. An instance without a check has nothing to audit. Your week 9 studio task may have come from your project. If so, its unattended attempt is a good instance. The final project's evaluation script must score at least one unattended attempt. Auditing that attempt now shows whether its check can fail, before the final depends on it.

    The accounting in step 5 uses the course-written script from the lecture. Open a shell at the course checkout and store the script's path in a variable. Keep the same shell for the rest of the studio:

    AUDIT_TOOL="$PWD/lectures/week-10/fixture/audit.py"

    Change to your project repository. Every later command runs there. If you did steps 1 and 2 in an earlier shell, run the line above again at the course checkout before step 3.

    Studio sequence

    Steps 1 and 2 are preparation. Do them before the session, in about 15 minutes, when you choose the instance. That frees the session's first 15 minutes for a timed, closed-book attempt at the first half of the CP2 practice paper. The paper has the format of next week's checkpoint. The other steps keep their times. Sit the second half at home in another 15 minutes. Then mark both halves against the paper's answers page. If you arrive without steps 1 and 2, do them in those first 15 minutes. In that case, sit the whole practice paper at home.

    Minutes Activity Evidence to keep
    Before 1. Name the instance and the claim Revision, task, check, claim
    Before 2. Which grader decides this instance? One sentence
    0–15 First half of the CP2 practice paper, timed and closed book Your script, kept to mark at home
    15–35 3. Can the check fail? One wrong change and what the check said
    35–50 4. Was the pass earned? One fresh input and its result, or "cannot tell"
    50–70 5. Is the denominator complete? The attempt table and the accounting output
    70–80 6. pass@k, or why it is undefined n, c, k and the figure or the reason
    80–90 7. Decision and exchange The four-line decision and one question from a peer

    1. Name the instance and the claim (10 minutes)

    Do this step and the next before the session. Record the revision you are auditing and whether the tree is clean:

    git rev-parse HEAD
git status --short

    The first command prints one commit hash. The second prints nothing when the tree is clean. If it lists files, commit them or set them aside first. Then the audit describes a revision that someone else can check out.

    In your work record, write four lines:

    • the task as it was given to the agent;
    • the exact command that checks it;
    • how many attempts were declared;
    • the claim you are auditing, in one sentence, for example "the agent fixed the date parser; the tests pass".

    Then write a prediction: which of the three questions do you expect to change that claim?

    2. Which grader decides this instance? (5 minutes)

    Write one sentence. Is your instance decided by code, by a person, or by a model judge? Is that the simplest grader that can decide it? If a model judges anything in your project, add three facts: where its labelled calibration cases are, whether the rubric was frozen before the last test, and which cases were held out. If there are no such cases, say so. That is a finding.

    3. Can the check fail? (20 minutes)

    Give the check a result you know is wrong. Do it in a separate worktree so that your working tree stays as recorded:

    git worktree add --detach ../week10-audit-scratch HEAD

    Preparing worktree (detached HEAD 347c1a7)
HEAD is now at 347c1a7 practice: first commit

    Your hash and commit subject will differ. Change to ../week10-audit-scratch and first run the instance's check command with nothing edited, exactly as written in step 1. It must give the same result as in your working tree, normally a pass. Sometimes it does not. A new worktree holds only committed files, so ignored files are absent: installed dependencies, build output and local settings. In that case, prepare the worktree the way your project prepares a fresh clone, then rerun. A failure before any change is a setup gap, not a finding.

    Then make one small wrong change to the code the task was about: return a constant, drop a condition, or swap a comparison. Run the check command again and record its exit status.

    • The check fails. It can detect this defect. Write down which defect. That is all the result shows.
    • The check still passes. First confirm that the check ran your change. Break the changed file so that it cannot load, for example with a syntax error, and rerun. If the check fails now, it does run that file: undo the break and keep the wrong change. If it still passes, it did not load that file from the scratch worktree. A common cause is a package installed in editable mode. The check then imports that package from your working tree. In that case, make your wrong changes in the working tree instead and run the check there. Undo each change with git restore followed by the file's path, so that git status --short prints nothing again. Suppose the check has run your wrong change and still passed. Then you have found a wrong result that the check accepts. Write the smallest case that would catch it. Do not add it to the project yet. Note it as a proposed repair, together with the wrong change that motivated it.

    Try a second wrong change that targets a different clause of the task. You may ask your agent to propose wrong changes. You choose which ones to run, and you read each one before you run it. Then change back to your project repository and remove the scratch worktree. Do not run the command from inside the scratch worktree: it would delete the directory your shell is in:

    git worktree remove --force ../week10-audit-scratch

    4. Was the pass earned? (15 minutes)

    Open the change the agent made for one attempt that passed. Without running anything, answer in one sentence: what does this change compute? Then take one input from the task statement that the check never showed the change. Run the change on it, and record the expected and observed result.

    Write one of three judgments, with the evidence beside it:

    Judgment Evidence to record
    Earned The fresh input, the expected result and the observed result
    Not earned What the change does instead: a constant, a special case for the checked input, an edited test, a printed success marker
    Cannot tell What the record lacks, such as the diff or the command output, and where you looked

    If the record shows the agent reading tests, history or another answer, quote what the task allowed before you judge. A cue is a reason to look, not a conclusion.

    5. Is the denominator complete? (20 minutes)

    Write every declared attempt into a table, including the ones with no result. Start from this template. It describes three declared attempts: one pass, one timeout, and one that left no record:

    mkdir -p week10-audit
cat > week10-audit/instance.json <<'__TABLE__'
{
  "protocol": {"tasks": ["my-instance"], "attempts_per_task": 3},
  "published_headline": "replace with the claim you are auditing",
  "rows": [
    {"task": "my-instance", "attempt": 1, "state": "completed", "recorded": "pass"},
    {"task": "my-instance", "attempt": 2, "state": "timeout", "recorded": null}
  ]
}
__TABLE__
python "$AUDIT_TOOL" headline --runs week10-audit/instance.json
python "$AUDIT_TOOL" denominator --runs week10-audit/instance.json

    {"claim": "replace with the claim you are auditing", "kind": "headline_as_published", "passed": 1, "percent": 100.0, "rate": "1/1", "scored_rows": 1}
{"accounted_for": 3, "declared": 3, "fail": 0, "kind": "as_recorded", "missing": 1, "not_earned": 0, "pass": 1, "pass_rate_if_unresolved_fail": "1/3", "pass_rate_if_unresolved_pass": "2/3", "timeout": 1, "unknown": 0}
{"attempts": ["pass", "timeout", "missing"], "task": "my-instance"}
{"accounted_for": 3, "declared": 3, "fail": 0, "kind": "audited", "missing": 1, "not_earned": 0, "pass": 1, "pass_rate_if_unresolved_fail": "1/3", "pass_rate_if_unresolved_pass": "2/3", "timeout": 1, "unknown": 0}

    The template's headline is 1 of 1. Its denominator is 1 of 3, with one attempt unresolved. Now replace the template with your own record:

    • set attempts_per_task to the number of attempts that were declared before the run, not the number that left a row;
    • write one row per attempt that left a record: state is completed or timeout, and recorded is pass, fail, or null when the attempt completed without a verdict;
    • write no row for an attempt that left no record, so that it is counted as missing.

    Run the two commands again. For your table the audited row repeats the as_recorded row, because the script knows nothing about your check. Apply your own findings by hand. Step 3 may have shown that a recorded pass was a wrong result, or step 4 that it was not earned. If so, change that row's recorded value to fail and add a "note" field saying why. Then rerun. Keep both outputs.

    6. pass@k, or why it is undefined (10 minutes)

    Run the calculation on your table. For the unedited template it prints:

    python "$AUDIT_TOOL" passk --runs week10-audit/instance.json

    {"n": 3, "pass@1": {"high": "2/3", "low": "1/3"}, "pass@2": {"high": "1", "low": "2/3"}, "passes": 1, "task": "my-instance", "unresolved": 1}
{"kind": "mean_over_declared_tasks", "pass@1": {"high": "2/3", "low": "1/3"}, "pass@2": {"high": "1", "low": "2/3"}, "tasks": 1}

    Before reading your own output, compute pass@1 and pass@2 by hand from n and c. Three cases need a sentence instead of a number:

    • With one declared attempt, the script prints "pass@2": "undefined: k exceeds n". Report pass@1 with n = 1 beside it and stop there.
    • If a later attempt saw the earlier failure, the attempts are not independent. Report the first attempt alone and describe the retry as a separate procedure.
    • If an attempt is unresolved, report the range.

    7. Decide and exchange (10 minutes)

    Finish with four lines:

    Supported:          <the claim your evidence supports, with its denominator>
Not supported:      <at least two claims the record cannot carry>
Decision:           accept | narrow | recheck | stop (evidence: <outputs or files>)
Decide again when:  <the check, the table or the task statement changes>

    Exchange records with one other student, in person or in writing. Ask one question about their denominator and one about their wrong change. Add the question you received and your answer to your record.

    What to keep

    Keep one plain-text record. It holds the revision, the four lines from step 1, both wrong changes and what the check said, and the earned judgment. It also holds week10-audit/instance.json with both accounting outputs, the pass@k line and the decision. Keep keys, private data and raw chat logs out of it.

    This record feeds the final project's evaluation item. The evaluation script reports the same accounting over your project's own tasks. It declares a number of attempts in advance. It counts each attempt as pass, fail, timeout, unknown or missing. It also includes one deliberately wrong candidate that its scorer must fail, like the wrong changes you gave the check in step 3. Add further tasks to this table rather than starting again, and declare each task's attempts before they run. You choose whether to commit week10-audit/ to your project. Your final can cite it either way. If your instance came from an open-source contribution, the final still needs tasks from your project.

    Participation and accessibility

    Everything here is plain text and can be done with a keyboard. A written or asynchronous exchange replaces the discussion in step 7 with the same evidence. If a command fails, keep the failure in the record and ask for help with the exact output. Record a step you could not run as not run.
