Agentic Software EngineeringBuild, verify, evaluate.

Agents generate. You verify, decide and explain.

A coding agent proposes a change; you decide whether the evidence supports it and whether you can explain it. Every example shows generation and verification. A test you can rerun is evidence; an agent saying “done” is a claim to check.

How this course works

  • Length: 12 teaching weeks, then an assessment week (expo and defenses) in week 13.
  • Two sessions a week: the first is a lecture that generates and then verifies, live. The second is a practitioner guest; in a week without a guest it is a studio, where you work on your own project or contribution while staff circulate. Each session is 90 minutes; the plan is one cohort of 60 students.
  • Three lines of work: six assignments (mostly in the first six weeks), four open-source contributions (across two repositories), and one term project (one or two people).

Twelve teaching weeks, then assessment

The course follows the public Fall 2026 calendar of Stanford CS146S, with two verification weeks added. Each teaching week has two 90-minute sessions: a lecture with live generation and verification, then a practitioner guest or a studio.

Explain your work without AI

CP1 in week 6 and CP2 in week 11 are thirty-minute, in-class paper checkpoints, each worth 7.5 points. The week 13 defense is worth 15 points: without a device, respond to a change or fault introduced in your own code during a fifteen-minute sitting.

Practise making a decision

Save a prediction before discussion, inspect a candidate or trace, run a discriminating check, revise after feedback and try a changed input. After checking, choose whether to accept, narrow, recheck or stop.

Syllabus at a glance

What you will be able to do

  • LO1: Explain a coding agent's loop, system prompt and tool definitions, and predict how it fails on context, tools and stopping.
  • LO2: Design an agent development workflow: context and specifications, MCP tools, skills, instruction files, hooks, subagents and background tasks.
  • LO3: Write a requirement as a specification an agent can implement and a test can check, and revise it when new evidence arrives.
  • LO4: Make an unfamiliar real codebase agent-ready, and deliver a change in it without breaking callers.
  • LO5: Verify agent-written code with regression and mutation tests, code review, static analysis and security scanning, and defend each acceptance.
  • LO6: Evaluate agents: check that a test can fail, that the denominator is complete and that a judge is reliable, and read small-sample results correctly.
  • LO7: Recognise and mitigate agent security risks (prompt injection, credentials, supply chain), verifying a defense with a local reproduction and a regression.
  • LO8: Describe how a team operates agents (portals, gateways, routing, cost), and how to recover from an incident and prove the recovery with a regression.
  • LO9: Collaborate responsibly: follow upstream rules, disclose AI use honestly, submit no low-effort generated work, and decide again when the inputs change.
